\begin{abstract}
We present a \textbf{speculative but quantitative} extension of QSD to the cosmological constant problem. The central observation is that syndrome extraction---the irreversible measurement process at the heart of QSD's error-correcting spacetime---carries a thermodynamic cost governed by Landauer's principle. The entropy production rate per Planck 4-volume is set by the graph strain: $S_{\text{comp}} = n/\gs$ nats, where $n = 4$ is the spacetime dimension. The vacuum energy density is the Planck density suppressed by the Boltzmann factor for this entropy cost, divided by a combinatorial prefactor $\Ncomb = n \times |E(K_4)| \times 2^k = 48$ that counts the independent dissipative channels:
\[
    \rhoL = \frac{\Mpl^4}{\Ncomb} \times e^{-n/\gs}
\]
Numerically, $4/\gs \approx 279.3$, giving $\rhoL/\Mpl^4 \approx 1.09 \times 10^{-123}$, compared to the observed $\rhoL/\Mpl^4 \approx 1.13 \times 10^{-123}$---agreement to 3.5\% across 123 orders of magnitude. The residual vacuum energy drives metric expansion as the only available mechanism for a closed system to accommodate monotonically increasing computational entropy. This extension uses no additional free parameters beyond those already present in QSD, but the derivation rests on physical arguments that are not yet rigorous, and we present it as a conjecture meriting further investigation.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Status: Speculative.  All results in this supplement are conjectural and do not affect the established predictions of the QSD core framework (S1--S6).}
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation: Spacetime as a Computer}
%=====================================================================

QSD treats spacetime as a quantum error-correcting code---specifically, the $[[5,1,3]]$ perfect code, whose graph structure produces the complete graph $K_4$ and the cycle graph $C_5$. Within this framework, physical processes are computational processes: gauge interactions arise from syndrome extraction on $K_4$, and geometric dynamics arise from the spectral structure of $C_5$.

Every real computer dissipates heat as it computes. This is not an engineering limitation but a fundamental consequence of the second law of thermodynamics, formalised by Landauer's principle \cite{Landauer1961}: erasing one bit of information requires at least $k_B T \ln 2$ of energy dissipated as heat. If spacetime \emph{is} a computer---as QSD proposes---then the continuous error-correction process must produce entropy. The question is: where does this computational heat go?

In an ordinary computer, heat flows to the environment. But the universe \emph{has} no environment. For a closed system, the only way to accommodate monotonically increasing entropy is to expand the available phase space. For spacetime, this means metric expansion.

\begin{observation}[Computational heat $\to$ dark energy]
If the universe continuously performs error correction on its own quantum-geometric code, the thermodynamic cost of this computation may manifest as the cosmological constant, and the metric expansion of space may be the physical mechanism by which the resulting entropy is accommodated.
\end{observation}

This observation connects three of the deepest problems in physics---the cosmological constant, the arrow of time, and the nature of quantum gravity---through a single mechanism: the thermodynamic cost of spacetime error correction. We develop this idea quantitatively in the following sections.

\section{Landauer's Principle and Holographic Bit Density}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Landauer's Principle}

Landauer's principle \cite{Landauer1961} states that the erasure of one bit of information in a system at temperature $T$ requires a minimum energy dissipation of:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:landauer}
    E_{\text{bit}} = k_B T \ln 2
\end{equation}

This is not a practical limitation but a \emph{thermodynamic identity}: information erasure is logically irreversible, and the second law demands entropy increase $\Delta S \geq k_B \ln 2$ per bit erased.

\subsection{Connection to $\Hbfour$}

The holographic bit density in $n$ dimensions (see S2 and the Redefinition Note) is:
\begin{equation}
    \Hbn = \frac{1}{n \ln 2}
\end{equation}

For $n = 4$:
\begin{equation}
    \Hbfour = \frac{1}{4 \ln 2} \approx 0.3607
\end{equation}

The $\ln 2$ in the denominator is \emph{the same} $\ln 2$ that appears in Landauer's principle. This is not a coincidence: $\Hbfour$ was derived from the Bekenstein--Hawking entropy bound $S = A/(4\ell_P^2)$, which counts the maximum number of bits encodable on a surface. The Landauer cost per bit is $k_B T \ln 2$; the information capacity per Planck area per dimension is $1/(n \ln 2)$. The two are conjugate quantities:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hb-landauer}
    \Hbn \times \ln 2 = \frac{1}{n}
\end{equation}

This identity states that the Landauer energy cost of encoding one holographic bit, measured in units of $k_B T$, is exactly $1/n$ per dimension. The total cost across all $n$ dimensions is unity.

\begin{remark}
Equation~\eqref{eq:hb-landauer} is an exact identity, not an approximation. It shows that $\Hbn$ already encodes the thermodynamic cost of information processing at the Planck scale.
\end{remark}

\section{Irreversibility of Syndrome Extraction}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Unitary Evolution vs.\ Syndrome Measurement}

In the QSD framework, the dynamics of spacetime involve two qualitatively different operations:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Unitary evolution} (Hamiltonian propagation on $K_4 \cup C_5$): This is reversible and generates no entropy.
    \item \textbf{Syndrome extraction} (measurement of stabiliser eigenvalues): This is \emph{irreversible}---it projects quantum superpositions onto definite syndrome outcomes, collapsing quantum information into classical syndrome bits.
\end{enumerate}

It is the second process---syndrome extraction---that carries Landauer cost. In the $[[5,1,3]]$ code, there are 4 stabiliser generators, each yielding one classical bit of syndrome information per measurement cycle. This is the same process that, in the QSD particle-physics sector, produces observable syndromes at $\Esyn = 28.24$~GeV (see S5).

\subsection{Error Rate and Correction Frequency}

The graph strain $\gs = 3/(80\varphi^2) \approx 0.01432$ sets the fractional error rate per coding cycle. In each Planck time interval, the probability that a given degree of freedom experiences an error requiring correction is proportional to $\gs$.

The \emph{number} of syndrome extractions per degree of freedom needed to accumulate one full bit of error entropy is therefore:
\begin{equation}
    N_{\text{cycle}} \sim \frac{1}{\gs}
\end{equation}

This means the syndrome measurement apparatus is continuously active, but produces irreversible entropy at rate $\gs$ per Planck time per degree of freedom.

\section{Entropy Production Rate}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Counting Irreversible Operations}

Consider one Planck 4-volume ($\ell_P^3 \times t_P$). The error-correcting code operates on this volume, and the entropy production has contributions from each spacetime dimension.

Per dimension, the holographic encoding capacity is $\Hbn$ bits per Planck area. The error rate per cycle is $\gs$. But the relevant quantity for the total entropy cost is not the per-cycle rate but the accumulated entropy barrier: how many nats of entropy separate the ``fully corrected'' vacuum from the ``uncorrected'' state.

\begin{proposition}[Computational entropy per Planck 4-volume]\label{prop:entropy}
The total computational entropy cost of maintaining the error-correcting code in $n$ spacetime dimensions is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Scomp}
    S_{\text{comp}} = \frac{n}{\gs} \quad \text{nats}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Argument]
Each of the $n$ spacetime dimensions requires independent error correction (the code protects geometric information along each axis). Per dimension, the number of syndrome extraction cycles needed to accumulate one nat of entropy is $1/\gs$ (since each cycle produces $\gs$ nats of irreversible entropy). The total across $n$ dimensions is $n/\gs$.
\end{proof}

For $n = 4$:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\text{comp}} = \frac{4}{\gs} = \frac{4 \times 80\varphi^2}{3} 
    = \frac{320\varphi^2}{3} \approx 279.26 \;\text{nats}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Interpretation}

The quantity $S_{\text{comp}} \approx 279$ nats is the \emph{entropy barrier} that separates the Planck-scale vacuum energy density from the observed vacuum energy density. It is large (explaining why $\Lambda$ is small) but finite and calculable from graph topology (explaining why $\Lambda$ is not zero).

\section{The Cosmological Constant}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Boltzmann Suppression}

In statistical mechanics, a state separated from the ground state by an entropy cost $S$ is suppressed by the Boltzmann factor $e^{-S}$. The vacuum energy density is the Planck density suppressed by the computational entropy barrier, divided by the number of independent dissipative channels through which the computational entropy is produced:

\begin{conjecture}[Computational vacuum energy]\label{conj:cc}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rhoL}
    \boxed{\rhoL = \frac{\Mpl^4}{\Ncomb} \times e^{-n/\gs}}
\end{equation}
where $\Mpl = \sqrt{\hbar c / G} \approx 1.221 \times 10^{19}$~GeV is the (unreduced) Planck mass, $n = 4$ is the spacetime dimension, $\gs = 3/(80\varphi^2)$ is the graph strain, and $\Ncomb = 48$ is the combinatorial prefactor derived in \S\ref{sec:prefactor}.
\end{conjecture}

\subsection{The Combinatorial Prefactor}\label{sec:prefactor}

The Boltzmann weight $e^{-n/\gs}$ gives the suppression per dissipative channel. The total vacuum energy density is this weight distributed across $\Ncomb$ independent channels, each carrying a fraction $1/\Ncomb$ of the Planck density. The channel count is:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ncomb}
    \Ncomb = n \times |E(K_4)| \times 2^k = 4 \times 6 \times 2 = 48
\end{equation}

The three factors have clear physical origins within the QSD framework:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item $n = 4$: the number of spacetime dimensions, each requiring independent error correction.
    \item $|E(K_4)| = 6$: the number of edges of the gauge graph $K_4$, which are the channels through which syndrome information propagates (each edge corresponds to a Heisenberg exchange bond).
    \item $2^k = 2$: the dimension of the logical Hilbert space of the $[[5,1,3]]$ code ($k = 1$ logical qubit), representing the two degenerate logical ground states between which the vacuum energy is partitioned.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}
The prefactor $\Ncomb = 48$ is twice the order of $S_4$ ($|S_4| = 24$), and can equivalently be written as $48 = 2 \times |S_4|$. It also equals $48 = 4!/2! \times 4$, though whether this alternative factorisation has physical significance is unclear.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Numerical Evaluation}

The exponent:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{4}{\gs} = \frac{320\varphi^2}{3} = \frac{160(3+\sqrt{5})}{3} \approx 279.26
\end{equation}

Converting to base 10:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{4/\gs}{\ln 10} = \frac{279.26}{2.3026} \approx 121.28
\end{equation}

Therefore:
\begin{equation}
    e^{-4/\gs} \approx 5.25 \times 10^{-122}
\end{equation}

With the combinatorial prefactor:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\rhoL}{\Mpl^4} = \frac{e^{-4/\gs}}{48} \approx \frac{5.25 \times 10^{-122}}{48} \approx 1.094 \times 10^{-123}
\end{equation}

\section{Residual Analysis: Code Uncertainty}
%=====================================================================

Given the $3.5\%$ residual, it is natural to ask whether the $[[5,1,3]]$ code's irreducible quantum uncertainty---the logical error rate---accounts for the gap.

\subsection{Logical Error Rate}

The $[[5,1,3]]$ code corrects $t = \lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor = 1$ error. An uncorrectable error requires $\geq 2$ simultaneous faults. For a depolarising channel with physical error rate $p = \gs$, the leading-order logical error rate is:
\begin{equation}
    P_L \approx \binom{5}{2}\,\gs^2 = 10\,\gs^2 \approx 2.1 \times 10^{-3}
\end{equation}

The full probability of 2 or more errors:
\begin{equation}
    P_{\text{fail}} = \sum_{j=2}^{5} \binom{5}{j}\,\gs^j(1-\gs)^{5-j} \approx 2.0 \times 10^{-3}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Effect on the Prediction}

If the logical error rate contributed a multiplicative correction $(1 + P_L)$ to the vacuum energy (representing the small probability that an uncorrectable error leaks additional entropy into the vacuum), the correction would be:
\begin{equation}
    (1 + P_L) \approx 1.002 \quad \text{i.e.\ $+0.2\%$}
\end{equation}

This is a factor of $\sim\!17$ too small to account for the 3.5\% residual. The code's intrinsic quantum uncertainty therefore contributes a real but subdominant correction.

\subsection{Interpretation}

The 3.5\% residual is \emph{not} explained by the code's logical error rate ($P_L \approx 0.2\%$ vs.\ the needed $3.5\%$). However, it \emph{is} comfortably within the observational uncertainty on $H_0$ (Remark~\ref{rem:obs-uncertainty}). We therefore cannot distinguish at present between:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item The formula being exact, with the residual absorbed by measurement uncertainty;
    \item An additional $O(\gs)$ correction to the combinatorial prefactor that has not yet been identified.
\end{enumerate}

Either way, the agreement to 3.5\% across 123 orders of magnitude is the central result.

\section{The Expansion Mechanism}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Why Expansion?}

The irreversible entropy production from syndrome extraction must be accommodated somewhere. For a system with an external environment, entropy can be exported as heat. But the universe is a closed system---there is no external thermal reservoir.

The second law requires the total entropy of a closed system to be non-decreasing. For a system at maximum entropy density, the only way to accommodate additional entropy is to increase the volume of the system, thereby increasing the total entropy capacity.

\begin{proposition}[Expansion as entropy accommodation]
For a closed system producing entropy at a rate $\dot{S}_{\text{comp}}$ per unit volume, the metric must expand at a rate sufficient to maintain quasi-equilibrium:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\dot{V}}{V} \geq \frac{\dot{S}_{\text{comp}}}{s_{\max}}
\end{equation}
where $s_{\max}$ is the maximum entropy density (set by the holographic bound).
\end{proposition}

This identifies the Hubble expansion rate $H = \dot{a}/a$ with the computational entropy production rate. Dark energy is not a mysterious substance---it is the thermodynamic cost of spacetime maintaining its own quantum coherence.

\subsection{Self-Consistency with de Sitter Temperature}

A de Sitter universe with Hubble parameter $H$ has a horizon temperature:
\begin{equation}
    \TdS = \frac{\hbar H}{2\pi k_B}
\end{equation}

The Landauer cost per bit at this temperature is:
\begin{equation}
    E_{\text{Landauer}} = k_B \TdS \ln 2 = \frac{\hbar H \ln 2}{2\pi}
\end{equation}

And the vacuum energy density from the Friedmann equation is:
\begin{equation}
    \rhoL = \frac{3H^2 \Mpl^2}{8\pi}
\end{equation}

Equating with our computational expression $\rhoL = (\Mpl^4/\Ncomb)\,e^{-4/\gs}$ gives a self-consistent $H$:
\begin{equation}
    H = \Mpl\sqrt{\frac{8\pi}{3\,\Ncomb}} \; e^{-2/\gs}
\end{equation}

The square root arises because $\rhoL \propto H^2$ (Friedmann), while $\rhoL \propto e^{-4/\gs}$ (Landauer--Boltzmann). The system selects the self-consistent solution automatically. That $H$ is exponentially small in $1/\gs$ is the correct qualitative behaviour.

\section{What This Would Resolve}
%=====================================================================

If Conjecture~\ref{conj:cc} is correct, it addresses three longstanding problems simultaneously:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The cosmological constant problem.} The standard question is: why is $\rhoL/\Mpl^4 \sim 10^{-123}$ rather than $O(1)$? In QFT, this requires cancellation of $\sim 10^{123}$ zero-point contributions. In QSD, the smallness has two sources: an exponential Boltzmann suppression $e^{-4/\gs} \sim 10^{-121}$ (naturally small because the entropy barrier $4/\gs \approx 279$ is naturally large) and a modest combinatorial factor $1/48$ (counting dissipative channels). Together they produce $10^{-123}$ from topology alone.

    \item \textbf{The coincidence problem.} Why is $\rhoL \sim \rho_{\text{matter}}$ today? In QSD, both the matter sector and the vacuum energy arise from the same graph strain $\gs$: matter masses come from the code-scale hierarchy (Main Preprint \S4), while $\rhoL$ comes from the computational entropy cost. The ``coincidence'' reflects the fact that a single parameter governs both scales.

    \item \textbf{The arrow of time.} Irreversible syndrome extraction produces entropy monotonically. Metric expansion accommodates this entropy. Time's arrow is thus grounded in the computational irreversibility of error correction, not in special initial conditions.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and Open Questions}
%=====================================================================

We emphasise that this supplement is speculative. Key limitations:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The entropy-counting argument is heuristic.} Proposition~\ref{prop:entropy} gives $S_{\text{comp}} = n/\gs$ by dimensional counting, not by explicit computation from the stabiliser algebra. A rigorous derivation should compute the von Neumann entropy production per syndrome extraction cycle from the $[[5,1,3]]$ stabiliser group and sum over all stabiliser generators and all Planck cells.

    \item \textbf{The Boltzmann interpretation is non-standard.} Applying the Boltzmann suppression $e^{-S}$ to vacuum energy density is motivated by analogy with statistical mechanics, but a cosmological ``partition function'' from which this factor emerges has not been constructed. The argument is plausible but not proven.

    \item \textbf{The combinatorial prefactor needs derivation.} The factor $\Ncomb = 48 = n \times |E(K_4)| \times 2^k$ is identified numerically and has a natural combinatorial interpretation, but has not been derived from first principles. In particular, the argument for \emph{dividing} by $\Ncomb$ (rather than multiplying, or taking a different power) rests on the physical picture of independent dissipative channels, which requires formalisation.

    \item \textbf{The 3.5\% residual is unresolved.} It falls within the observational uncertainty on $H_0$ ($\sim\!2\%$ propagated to $\rhoL$), so it may not require a theoretical explanation. But if future measurements tighten $H_0$ and the residual persists, an additional correction of order $O(\gs)$ would need to be identified. The code's logical error rate ($P_L \approx 0.2\%$) is too small by a factor of $\sim\!17$ to be the source (\S6).

    \item \textbf{Backreaction is ignored.} We treat $\gs$ as constant, but in principle the computational entropy production could modify the effective graph strain at cosmological scales. Whether $\gs$ runs with the scale factor is an open question.

    \item \textbf{Relation to other approaches.} The Landauer mechanism has conceptual overlap with Padmanabhan's emergent gravity programme \cite{Padmanabhan2010} and with Verlinde's entropic gravity \cite{Verlinde2011}. Whether QSD provides a microscopic completion of these ideas, or is an independent framework, remains to be established.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Summary}
%=====================================================================

We have proposed a speculative but quantitative connection between QSD's error-correcting spacetime and the cosmological constant:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Syndrome extraction is irreversible (Landauer's principle applies).
    \item The entropy production rate per Planck 4-volume is $4/\gs \approx 279$ nats.
    \item The vacuum energy is Planck-density suppressed by $e^{-4/\gs}$, divided by the combinatorial channel count $\Ncomb = n \times |E(K_4)| \times 2^k = 48$.
    \item The prediction $\rhoL/\Mpl^4 \approx 1.09 \times 10^{-123}$ matches observation ($1.13 \times 10^{-123}$) to 3.5\%.
    \item The mechanism requires \emph{zero} additional parameters beyond those already in QSD.
    \item Metric expansion is the physical mechanism accommodating computational entropy in a closed universe.
\end{enumerate}

If correct, this resolves the cosmological constant problem, the coincidence problem, and the arrow of time within a single framework. The key insight is that $\Hbfour = 1/(4\ln 2)$ already contains Landauer's $\ln 2$---the holographic bit density and the thermodynamic cost of computation are two faces of the same constant.

\bigskip
\noindent\textit{The vacuum energy is the Planck density, attenuated by the computational cost of running the universe.}
